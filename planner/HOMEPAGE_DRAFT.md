# Ugly Manling Homepage Draft

## Purpose
This homepage should do three things immediately:
1. Communicate what Ugly Manling is and why it matters.
2. Show the core offerings clearly.
3. Help users quickly get to the thing they care most about.

The homepage should not feel like:
1. a blog,
2. a generic health landing page,
3. a disconnected storefront,
4. a vague brand splash page.

It should feel like a sharp front door into a useful platform for balding men.

## Core Homepage Principle
The homepage should behave like an intent router.

It should:
1. make the value obvious,
2. reduce confusion fast,
3. route users into the right path,
4. keep the assessment as the primary conversion anchor.

## Primary Conversion Goal
Primary CTA across the homepage:
1. `Start your assessment`

Why:
1. The assessment is the best first step for unsure users.
2. It powers personalization.
3. It routes users into the broader offering structure.
4. It is the strongest front door into the platform.

## Homepage Structure
### 1. Hero
The hero should immediately explain the promise in one hard headline and one clean subhead.

Suggested direction:
1. Headline: `Balding? Get a real plan.`
2. Subhead: `Ugly Manling helps men understand where they are, what to do next, and which tools, experts, and products are actually worth their time.`

Hero CTAs:
1. Primary: `Start your assessment`
2. Secondary: `Explore offerings`

Hero goals:
1. Make the category obvious.
2. Make the value obvious.
3. Make the next action obvious.

### 2. Quick-Path Navigation
Directly under the hero, add a fast intent-selection section.

Suggested heading:
1. `What are you here for?`

Suggested quick paths:
1. `I want a plan`
2. `I want treatment help`
3. `I want a better haircut / style`
4. `I want to talk to an expert`
5. `I want products`
6. `I want research / answers`

Design intent:
1. Large, obvious, clickable cards or pills.
2. One click should route a user into the right path.
3. This is the speed layer for users who already know their intent.

Routing logic:
1. `I want a plan` -> assessment / planning flow
2. `I want treatment help` -> treatment and services
3. `I want a better haircut / style` -> barber and style support
4. `I want to talk to an expert` -> expert access
5. `I want products` -> shop
6. `I want research / answers` -> research and education

### 3. Core Offerings Grid
Below quick-path navigation, show the offering backbone of the platform.

Offerings:
1. Guidance and planning
2. Expert access
3. Treatment and service access
4. Barber and style support
5. Shop
6. Research and education

Each offering card should include:
1. A short one-line value statement
2. A specific CTA
3. A trust cue where useful

Suggested CTA pattern:
1. Guidance and planning -> `Get my plan`
2. Expert access -> `Book a consult`
3. Treatment and service access -> `See treatment options`
4. Barber and style support -> `Find my style`
5. Shop -> `Shop essentials`
6. Research and education -> `See what actually works`

Suggested card copy direction:
1. Guidance and planning: `Figure out where you are and what to do next.`
2. Expert access: `Talk to someone credible before you make the wrong move.`
3. Treatment and service access: `Cut through the noise and find real options.`
4. Barber and style support: `Look better fast, even before anything grows back.`
5. Shop: `Buy products that solve real problems, not junk.`
6. Research and education: `Understand what matters, what works, and what is hype.`

### 4. Why Ugly Manling
Add a short section that explains why the platform exists and why it is different.

Suggested framing:
1. `Most hair-loss advice is vague, scammy, or built to sell you fear.`
2. `Ugly Manling gives you clarity, options, and a real next step.`

This section should reinforce:
1. evidence-backed guidance,
2. confidence-first support,
3. practical action over fluff,
4. strong brand differentiation.

### 5. Community as a Horizontal Layer
Community should not appear as a giant standalone section.

Instead, it should show up as proof and support inside the homepage.

Suggested placements:
1. Success story snippets below the hero
2. Testimonials inside offering cards
3. Small proof modules near treatment and style sections
4. “People like you” trust snippets near major CTAs

Community should feel like:
1. validation,
2. social proof,
3. support,
4. proof that users are not alone.

### 6. How It Works
Add a simple step-by-step trust section that shows the user journey.

Suggested steps:
1. Assess
2. Get your plan
3. Choose your path
4. Take action
5. Come back stronger

Purpose:
1. Show that the platform is a system.
2. Reduce uncertainty.
3. Reinforce that every offering connects back to a coherent journey.

### 7. Footer CTA
End with a strong repeat CTA block.

Recommended CTA:
1. `Start your assessment`

Optional secondary CTA:
1. `Browse the shop`

Rule:
1. The homepage should always have a clear path back to assessment.

## Content and UX Rules
1. Do not make users read too much before acting.
2. The page should be scannable in seconds.
3. The hero and quick-path layer should do most of the routing work.
4. Offerings should reflect the product architecture, not random features.
5. Community should support trust across the page, not become a separate lane.

## Draft Copy Direction
Tone should be:
1. Bold
2. Direct
3. Useful
4. Slightly funny when it helps
5. Evidence-backed when claims are involved

The homepage should feel like:
1. a confident operator,
2. not a corporate wellness brand,
3. not a medical brochure,
4. not a dropship storefront.

## Build Notes
When this moves into UI design and implementation, prioritize:
1. strong hero hierarchy,
2. quick-path interaction design,
3. clear offering cards,
4. embedded trust modules,
5. repeated assessment CTA without feeling repetitive.

## Summary
The homepage should work like this:
1. State the promise.
2. Let the user self-select intent.
3. Show the offering backbone.
4. Reinforce trust.
5. Route the user into the right next step.
