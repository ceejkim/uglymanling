# Ugly Manling Brand System

## Purpose
This document is the canonical brand reference for Ugly Manling.

Use it to guide:
1. copy,
2. visuals,
3. UI design,
4. campaign concepts,
5. community messaging,
6. conversion language,
7. product storytelling.

This file defines what the brand should feel like and what it must never become.

## Brand Identity
Ugly Manling is a community-driven platform for balding men focused on:
1. confidence,
2. identity,
3. humor,
4. honesty,
5. real shared experience.

The brand must feel:
1. direct,
2. self-aware,
3. slightly irreverent,
4. authentic,
5. grounded in real human experience.

## Core Tone
Brutally honest, concise, and human, with controlled humor.

The goal is not to sound polished.
The goal is to sound real, sharp, and trustworthy.

## Tone Pillars
### 1. Direct
1. Say things plainly.
2. Avoid euphemisms.
3. Avoid fluff.

### 2. Slightly Irreverent
1. Humor should be sharp and intentional.
2. Humor should make the user feel seen, not mocked.
3. Never let humor overpower usefulness.

### 3. Authentic
1. Sound like a real person.
2. Avoid corporate language.
3. Avoid generic marketing jargon.

### 4. Purpose-Driven
1. Tie the brand back to confidence and identity.
2. Keep the work grounded in helping men make better next-step decisions.

## Tone Guardrails
### Do
1. Keep language tight and punchy.
2. Prioritize honesty over cleverness.
3. Sound like a confident peer.
4. Use humor to reduce anxiety.
5. Stay grounded in actual user experience.

### Do Not
1. Use corporate or polished marketing language.
2. Overuse humor.
3. Sound generically inspirational.
4. Shame or alienate the user.
5. Drift into macho posturing.
6. Drift into pity or sentimentality.
7. Drift into influencer-brand emptiness.

## Tone Failure Modes
Reject outputs that feel:
1. too clinical,
2. too harsh,
3. too polished,
4. too “wellness brand,”
5. too “alpha male,”
6. too “creator funnel,”
7. too cute,
8. too generic.

## Visual System
### Core Principle
Professional, clean, and structured execution with subtle, unexpected personality.

The brand should feel controlled and confident, not chaotic or gimmicky.

### Color Palette
Primary:
1. Duck Yellow: `#FFD84D`
2. Deep Blue: `#1F3A5F`

Secondary:
1. Soft Blue: `#E6F0FA`
2. Navy: `#0F1F2E`
3. Off-White: `#F7F6F2`

Accent:
1. Orange: `#FF8C42`
2. Gray: `#A7B0B8`

### Visual Rules
#### Layout
1. Clean.
2. Minimal.
3. Structured.
4. Grid-based.
5. Generous whitespace.

#### Imagery
1. Prefer real people over stock-photo energy.
2. Use natural lighting.
3. Slight imperfection is acceptable if it feels human and credible.

#### Duck Motif
1. The duck is now an intentional mascot system for Ugly Manling.
2. It should feel cute, angry, and oddly reassuring.
3. Use one primary duck mark repeatedly, and use scene illustrations as support.
4. The duck should add personality, not turn the product into a cartoon toy.
5. Keep mascot usage structured, restrained, and integrated with the layout.

#### Mascot System
1. Primary mascot: single angry duck used in header, hero, footer, and brand marks.
2. Secondary mascot scenes: mirror, shower, and group-duck illustrations used in community and story moments.
3. Use duck-derived iconography for community, honesty, self-check, and action cues when custom icons are needed.
4. Avoid scattering large mascot art in every section.

#### Typography
1. Use clean sans-serif typography.
2. Prioritize confident readability.
3. Do not use decorative fonts.

## UI Implications
The product should not look like:
1. a luxury grooming brand,
2. a sterile hospital portal,
3. a startup landing page template,
4. a meme account pretending to be software.

The product should look like:
1. a serious platform,
2. made by people with taste,
3. for men dealing with a real identity issue,
4. with enough personality to feel distinct.

## Approved Direction Examples
### Good
1. `Balding? Let's make a plan.`
2. `See what actually works.`
3. `Get real feedback.`
4. `Cut through the noise.`

### Bad
1. `Unlock your hair transformation journey.`
2. `Become your most confident self today.`
3. `Elite men choose this protocol.`
4. `We know balding can be hard, and we are here for you every step of the way.`

## CTA Style
CTAs should be:
1. clear,
2. blunt,
3. useful,
4. low-drama.

Examples:
1. `Start your assessment`
2. `Join our community`
3. `See treatment options`
4. `Find my style`
5. `Ask a question`

## Enforcement Standard
Any output that violates this system should be:
1. rejected,
2. rewritten,
3. or escalated to the `Orchestrator`.

When in doubt:
1. prioritize honesty over performance,
2. prioritize human tone over polish,
3. prioritize trust over cleverness.
