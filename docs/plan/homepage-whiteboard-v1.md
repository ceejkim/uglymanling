# Ugly Manling Homepage Whiteboard V1

## Goal
Design the first page we should build for Ugly Manling.

This homepage needs to:
1. communicate the purpose and core value immediately,
2. align to the brand style: direct, concise, self-aware, slightly irreverent, and trustworthy,
3. clearly show the core offerings,
4. give users a visible `Join our community` path and explain what the community is for,
5. include connection options now, even before Calendly is integrated,
6. route people quickly into the thing they care about most.

## Core Homepage Principle
This page should feel like a clear, high-trust front door for balding men.

It should not feel like:
1. a soft wellness brand,
2. a generic health-tech landing page,
3. a blog homepage,
4. a disconnected merch store.

It should say, fast:
1. here is what Ugly Manling is,
2. here is how we help,
3. here is your next move.

## 1. Hero
### Purpose
The hero should communicate the category, tone, and value within seconds.

### Headline Direction
Primary recommended line:
1. `Balding? Let's make a plan.`

Alternate line:
1. `Stop guessing. Start making smarter moves.`

### Subhead Direction
1. `Ugly Manling helps you figure out what stage you're in, what actually helps, and which next move is worth your time.`
2. `Less panic. Less nonsense. More clarity on treatment, style, products, and what actually matters.`

### Hero CTAs
1. Primary: `Start your assessment`
2. Secondary: `Join our community`
3. Tertiary: `Contact us`

### Hero Design Direction
1. Strong but clean headline hierarchy.
2. Structured, grid-based layout.
3. Generous whitespace.
4. One strong visual or photographic treatment, not stock-photo healthcare cliches.
5. The hero should feel confident and human, not theatrical.

## 2. Intent Router Strip
### Purpose
Help the user self-select their main need instantly.

### Section Heading
1. `What are you here for?`

### Quick Paths
1. `I want a plan`
2. `I want treatment help`
3. `I want a better haircut`
4. `I want to talk to an expert`
5. `I want products`
6. `I want answers`

### UX Direction
1. Use large clickable cards or clean structured pills.
2. Make this section highly scannable.
3. On mobile, this can become a horizontally scrollable rail.

### Routing Logic
1. `I want a plan` -> assessment / planning
2. `I want treatment help` -> treatment and services
3. `I want a better haircut` -> style support
4. `I want to talk to an expert` -> expert access
5. `I want products` -> shop
6. `I want answers` -> research and education

## 3. Core Offerings Grid
### Purpose
Show the actual platform backbone clearly.

### Offerings
1. Guidance and planning
2. Expert access
3. Treatment and service access
4. Barber and style support
5. Shop
6. Research and education

### Card Formula
Each card should include:
1. one-line value statement,
2. one clear CTA,
3. one trust cue or supporting line.

### Suggested CTA Pattern
1. Guidance and planning -> `Get my plan`
2. Expert access -> `Request a consult`
3. Treatment and service access -> `See treatment options`
4. Barber and style support -> `Find my style`
5. Shop -> `Shop essentials`
6. Research and education -> `See what actually works`

### Suggested Copy Direction
1. Guidance and planning: `Figure out where you are and what to do next.`
2. Expert access: `Talk to someone credible before you make an expensive mistake.`
3. Treatment and service access: `Cut through the noise and find real options.`
4. Barber and style support: `Look better fast, without the panic haircut.`
5. Shop: `Buy products that solve real problems, not fake-confidence junk.`
6. Research and education: `Understand what matters, what works, and what is hype.`

## 4. Community Band
### Purpose
Make community visible and valuable without turning the homepage into a social feed.

### Community CTA
1. `Join our community`

### What the Community Is For
The page should explain community as:
1. a place to ask honest questions,
2. a place to compare notes with other men going through it,
3. a place to share progress and wins,
4. a place to get feedback without shame,
5. a place to stop dealing with this alone.

### Suggested Copy Direction
1. `For men comparing notes, getting real feedback, and not doing this alone.`
2. `No shame. No hype. No weird miracle-hair cult energy.`

### Visual Treatment
1. Use a clean featured band or card.
2. Include short quotes, small stories, or “people like you” snippets.
3. Keep the section supportive, structured, and human.

## 5. How It Works
### Purpose
Explain the product as a coherent system.

### Steps
1. Assess
2. Get your plan
3. Choose your path
4. Take action
5. Come back stronger

### UX Direction
1. Make this visual and simple.
2. Use clear structure, icons, or numbered steps.
3. Reinforce that the product is not random content or disconnected tools.

## 6. Connection / Contact Options
### Purpose
Give users a way to reach out before Calendly is live.

### Options
1. `Contact us`
2. `Request a consult`
3. `Talk to the team`
4. `Join community`
5. `Scheduling coming soon`

### Product Intent
This section should act as a lightweight conversion fallback for users who want help now.

### UX Direction
1. Keep it compact.
2. Make it feel human, not corporate support-desk-ish.
3. Assume this initially routes to email, contact form, or placeholder consult intake.

## 7. Trust and Proof Strip
### Purpose
Earn trust without sounding clinical or weak.

### Inputs To Show
1. community quotes,
2. “people like you” snippets,
3. short evidence-backed guidance line,
4. testimonials embedded near relevant actions.

### Role
This is the proof layer that supports the whole page.

## 8. Footer CTA
### CTA Hierarchy
1. Primary: `Start your assessment`
2. Secondary: `Join our community`

### Rule
The page should always funnel people back toward a clear next step.

## CTA Hierarchy
1. `Start your assessment`
2. `Join our community`
3. `Explore offerings`
4. `Request a consult`
5. `Contact us`
6. contextual offering CTAs like `Get my plan` and `Find my style`

## Visual / UI Direction
### Overall Feel
1. Professional, clean, and structured.
2. Slightly irreverent, but controlled.
3. Human and grounded.
4. Trustworthy without becoming sterile or corporate.

### Typography
1. Use clean sans-serif typography.
2. Prioritize confident readability over decorative personality.

### Color Direction
1. Primary: Duck Yellow `#FFD84D` and Deep Blue `#1F3A5F`.
2. Secondary: Soft Blue `#E6F0FA`, Navy `#0F1F2E`, Off-White `#F7F6F2`.
3. Accent: Orange `#FF8C42`, Gray `#A7B0B8`.
4. Avoid luxury-grooming cliches, soft SaaS gradients, and purple startup defaults.

### Layout Direction
1. Clean, minimal, structured layouts.
2. Grid-based alignment.
3. Generous whitespace.
4. Personality should come through restraint and sharp copy, not visual chaos.
5. If the duck motif appears, it must be subtle and symbolic.

### Motion Direction
1. Keep motion light and intentional.
2. Use it to support clarity, not spectacle.
3. Avoid gimmicky animation.

## Build Plan For Page One
### Phase 1: Content and Structure
1. Lock hero copy.
2. Lock intent-router choices.
3. Lock offering-card copy and CTA mapping.
4. Define community explanation and placeholder contact language.

### Phase 2: Visual System
1. Choose fonts.
2. Define color system from `branding/BRAND_SYSTEM.md`.
3. Design hero composition.
4. Design card and button system.
5. Define community/proof modules.

### Phase 3: Frontend Implementation
1. Build hero and CTA block first.
2. Build intent router second.
3. Build offerings grid third.
4. Add community band and trust strips.
5. Add contact/connect panel with placeholder actions.
6. Finish with footer CTA.

### Phase 4: Integration Hooks
1. Wire `Start your assessment` to the future assessment flow.
2. Wire `Join our community` to a waitlist, email capture, or community entry path.
3. Wire `Contact us` and `Request a consult` to a basic contact form or email endpoint.
4. Leave clear placeholder hooks for Calendly.
5. Track CTA clicks from day one.

## Homepage-Specific Integrations To Prepare For
For this first page, we do not need every platform integration live yet.

What we should be ready to plug in:
1. `PostHog` for CTA and funnel analytics
2. `Resend` for community/signup/contact follow-up emails
3. `Clerk` later for authenticated community or assessment entry
4. `Calendly` later for actual consult booking
5. basic form handling for `Contact us` and `Request a consult`

## Summary
This homepage should behave like:
1. a clear statement of purpose,
2. a routing layer into the offering backbone,
3. a trust-building front door,
4. and a brand moment that feels unmistakably Ugly Manling.
